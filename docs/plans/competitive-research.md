# Competitive research plan

Date: September 15, 2026 (America/New_York).

## Problem and purpose

The user wants an evidence-based assessment of whether Reflow Studio can contend for a top-three finish and what work would most improve its chances. Current prototype checks establish limited correctness, not superiority to competing entries or real design tools. A weak comparison, outdated product claim, or speculative judging assumption could send the three-person team toward low-value work.

## Scope and approach

1. Read current repository status and distinguish delivered work, concurrent work, and proposals.
2. Retrieve the official event/technical brief where accessible; record inaccessible sources and unresolved language, runtime, model, and judging constraints.
3. Research current Adobe and competing product capabilities, comparable hackathon winners and judging commentary, and primary layout/reflow/evaluation research.
4. Map findings to concrete engine, typography, evaluation, integration, and demo improvements. Assign priorities and proposed owners using the existing team split.
5. Save a self-contained sourced research report with explicit decision gates, test-first acceptance criteria, and an honest competitiveness assessment.

## Risks

- Do not confuse the North America Chameleon event with Adobe India's similarly named event.
- Do not equate another event's winner or a paper's score with this competition's ranking.
- Separate source claims from our deductions; source reports and product marketing are not independent head-to-head tests.
- Avoid declaring existing native features novel or inferring that a missing public description means a feature does not exist.
- Preserve ongoing engine changes. This task owns only this plan and its research report; no production code or other task's tests.
- Keep the family business as presentation context, not a feature backlog or required pilot.

## Acceptance checks defined before writing the report

- Each material external claim has a direct primary-source link and source date/context where available.
- Clearly identify which official requirements were directly verified and which remain unverified.
- Include current capability/gap assessment, product and winner comparisons, and prioritized work with owner and measurable acceptance criteria.
- Distinguish proposed internal targets from Adobe thresholds; avoid unsupported odds or promises of winning.
- Check local Markdown links, the final diff, absence of private organizational research, and no changes to production code.
- Record research limitations and reusable-skill assessment. Documentation-only work requires content/link checks, not artificial executable tests.
